# TOPIC IDEAS — AI + LinkedIn

Her audience wants two things: **to be seen on LinkedIn**, and **to use AI and no-code tools
properly**. Her best topics sit where those two meet: AI used to get noticed.

This file is a bank and a method. The bank lasts months; the method lasts forever.

---

## The method

A topic is ready when it has **a number, a verb and an outcome**, and an obvious thing to light.

```
weak    AI for LinkedIn
weak    Best ChatGPT prompts
ready   6 AI workflows that get you noticed on LinkedIn          (lit: start here)
ready   Most people post. The top 10% get chosen.                (lit: biggest gap)
ready   5 AI tools ranked for LinkedIn creators                  (lit: #1)
```

Then three filters:

1. **Spotlight test.** Is there one obvious answer to light? If every item is equal, the topic
   is a list, not a sheet. Find the angle where one thing matters most.
2. **Tested test.** Can she say she used it? Her voice is "tested, not reported".
3. **Swap test.** Put another creator's name on it. Still fits? It is not hers yet.

## The eight lanes

Rotate lanes, not just topics.

### 1. Get noticed
LinkedIn visibility and positioning, with AI as the lever.
- 6 AI workflows that get you noticed on LinkedIn
- The profile section every visitor reads first, and almost nobody rewrites
- Why your posts get likes but no DMs
- What the top 10% do in the first hour after posting

### 2. AI tool truth
Practical, tested product insight: her "trusted voice" lane.
- 5 AI tools ranked for LinkedIn creators
- I tested [N] AI writing tools for a month. One stayed.
- What each AI model is actually best at for content
- The paid AI tool features worth paying for, and the ones that are not

### 3. No-code workflows
Systems a non-technical founder can build in an afternoon.
- The no-code stack that runs a creator's week
- Turn one post into five assets without opening a design tool
- Automate your content calendar with no code
- The 20-minute setup that saves a founder's Monday

### 4. Profile as a landing page
- Your LinkedIn profile is a landing page: audit it in 10 checks
- The headline formula for founders who want inbound
- What to put in your featured section (and what to remove)
- Banner, headline, about: the order people actually read them in

### 5. The content engine
AI-assisted creation that still sounds like you.
- Prompts that write in your voice, not ChatGPT's
- One idea, seven formats: the repurpose loop
- How to brief AI so it stops sounding like AI
- The carousel workflow, from outline to post

### 6. Founder growth
Personal brand as a business asset.
- Most people post. The top 10% get chosen.
- What founders with inbound pipelines do differently
- From 0 to first inbound lead: the 30-day plan
- Personal brand vs company page: where founders should post

### 7. Brand collaborations
Her edge, with 50+ brand partnerships behind it. A lane almost nobody in AI content can write.
- What brands look for before they pay a creator
- How to price your first LinkedIn brand deal
- The collaboration pitch that gets a reply
- Red flags in a brand deal, from 50+ of them

### 8. Myths vs reality
- LinkedIn myths that are quietly killing your reach
- AI content myths: what readers can actually tell
- "Post every day": true, false, or it depends?

## Before you commit

1. **Check `shipped-log.md`.** Anything close in the last month is a repeat.
2. **Check the lane rotation.** Three "AI tool truth" sheets in a row looks like a review site.
3. **Two sponsored sheets never run back to back.**
4. **Fact-check before writing.** Tools change monthly.

## Where new topics come from

1. **Her DMs and comments.** Questions people ask her are the topic list.
2. **What she tested this month.** First-hand beats researched, every time.
3. **A tool launch or a LinkedIn change.** Makes a lane-2 or lane-1 sheet urgent for a week.
4. **Her brand deals.** What a partner taught her about how brands choose creators.

Never from: a competitor's sheet, an AI news roundup, or a list of tools nobody has used.
