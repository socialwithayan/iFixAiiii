# TOPIC IDEAS

Her audience is one person: **a founder with too much to do, who does not enjoy tools.**
Every topic has to answer *what do I do Monday.*

This file is a bank and a method. Use the method when the bank runs dry — a list of fifty
topics lasts a year, a method lasts forever.

---

## The method

A topic is ready when it has **a number, a verb and an outcome.**

```
weak    AI tools for founders
weak    How to use Claude better
ready   The 5 systems that save a founder 10 hours a week
ready   The 30-minute setup that kills your Friday admin
ready   7 things you are still doing by hand that stopped being manual in 2025
```

Then check it against three filters:

1. **Monday test** — can they act on it this week without learning a new tool?
2. **Cost test** — does it name what the problem costs them now, in hours or money?
3. **Swap test** — put a competitor's name on it. Still fits? Then it is not a topic yet.

Anything that fails all three is a tweet, not a sheet.

## The eight lanes

Rotate lanes, not just topics. Four sheets in a row from the same lane makes a feed look
narrow even when every sheet is good.

### 1. Time back
The core lane. A named workflow, the hours it returns, what it replaces.

- 5 AI systems that save a founder 10 hours a week
- The Friday report that writes itself
- 7 hours of admin you can delete before lunch
- What a founder's week looks like before and after one afternoon of setup
- The three meetings that become a document instead

### 2. Manual vs system
Her strongest comparison lane. Two states, side by side.

- Manual founder vs system founder
- What you do 40 times a month that should run once
- Before and after: the inbox
- Copy-paste founder vs context founder
- The same tool, set up two ways

### 3. The setup
A build, step by step, in a fixed time box.

- Set up your first AI system in 30 minutes
- The 20-minute project setup that ends re-explaining yourself
- Your first automation, from nothing, in one sitting
- The five files every founder's AI workspace needs
- How to onboard a tool so it actually gets used

### 4. What it costs you
The cost of not having a system. Honest numbers only.

- What re-explaining your business costs you per month
- The real price of a tool nobody set up
- Why your team stopped using the thing you bought
- Four hours a week, priced at your own rate
- The admin tax on a founder's calendar

### 5. Stop doing this
The hard-truth lane. This is where the Ink canvas earns its one-in-ten.

- Stop hiring for work a system does
- You do not need another tool
- The automations that quietly cost more than they save
- Five things founders automate that should stay human
- Why your workflow broke the moment you scaled it

### 6. Founder's week
Time-shaped content. Schedules, routines, rhythms.

- A founder's Monday, systemised
- The 10 minutes that decide your week
- What runs while you sleep
- The weekly review that takes six minutes
- Your calendar, audited

### 7. Tool truth
Honest, specific, not a listicle of features.

- What each model is actually for
- The four connectors worth setting up, and the ones that are not
- When a prompt should become a saved system
- Free vs paid, for a founder specifically
- The feature you are paying for and not using

### 8. Delegation and team
Systems as a hiring alternative or a team multiplier.

- What to systemise before you hire
- Onboarding a new hire with the system you already built
- The handover document that writes itself
- Your first AI system your team actually uses
- What stays human when everything else runs

## Choosing a frame for a lane

| Lane | Usually |
|---|---|
| Time back | Rail |
| Manual vs system | Two-State Board |
| The setup | Clock or Pipeline |
| What it costs you | Funnel or Rail |
| Stop doing this | Rail on the Ink canvas |
| Founder's week | Clock or Loop |
| Tool truth | Ladder or Orbit |
| Delegation and team | Fork or Pipeline |

Not a rule, a starting point. The content decides the frame; this table just saves a minute.

## Before you commit

1. **Check `shipped-log.md`.** Anything close in the last month is a repeat, however fresh it
   feels while writing it.
2. **Check the lane rotation.** Three sheets from "Time back" in a row is a narrow feed.
3. **Fact-check the numbers before writing, not after.** If the honest total is 7 hours, the
   headline says seven — and the sheet is better for it.

## Where new topics come from

Best sources, in order:

1. **Her own replies.** What founders ask her in DMs and comments is a topic list written by
   the audience.
2. **The step people get stuck on.** Every system has one. That step is a whole sheet.
3. **What she built this month.** The thing she actually set up for herself is more credible
   than anything researched, because the numbers are hers.
4. **A tool changing.** A launch or a pricing change makes a lane-7 sheet urgent for a week.

Never from: a competitor's sheet, a trending hashtag, or a list of "AI tools" with no
workflow attached.
