## Who she is talking to

A founder with too much to do and no time to learn a tool for its own sake. They do not want
to be impressed. They want the thing that takes the Tuesday admin block off their calendar.

Everything follows from that:

- **Outcome before mechanism.** What it gives back, then how it works.
- **Their week, not the technology's roadmap.** "Your Friday report writes itself" beats
  "orchestrated multi-agent summarisation".
- **Never assume they enjoy this.** They are not going to tinker. It has to run.

## The sound

Calm and certain. She is not selling and she is not hyping — she already built the thing and
she is telling you what it does.

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Plain words. "Set up", not "leverage". "Runs", not "orchestrates".
- Second person. "You", "your inbox", "your Friday".
- Specific over impressive. "Two hours" beats "massive time savings" every time.
- Warm, not chummy. She is a peer who did the work, not a friend and not a guru.

## Banned

| Do not write | Because |
|---|---|
| "game-changer", "10x your", "unlock", "supercharge", "revolutionise" | hype words; her whole position is the opposite of hype |
| "insane", "crazy", "mind-blowing" | someone else's voice |
| "leverage", "utilise", "synergy", "seamless" | corporate filler |
| "just", "simply", "easy" | makes a founder who found it hard feel stupid |
| em dashes | she uses commas and full stops |
| emoji on the canvas | never, anywhere on a sheet |
| rhetorical questions as hooks | "Ever wish your inbox ran itself?" is not her |
| "AI will replace…" | fear framing, wrong audience |

## On the sheet

**Headline** — 8–12 words. One concrete number. One verb. One outcome.
The pill word carries the number or the outcome, never a filler word.

```
good   5 AI Systems That Save Founders [10 HOURS] A Week
good   The [30-MINUTE] Setup That Kills Your Friday Admin
weak   Powerful AI Workflows Every Founder Should Know
weak   Unlock The Full Potential Of Your AI Stack
```

**Deck** — one line that adds, never repeats.
`Not more tools. The same five workflows, running without you.`

**Card title** — 3–6 words. A thing, not a category. "Inbox triage and first drafts", not
"Email automation".

**Card body** — 18–30 words. What it does, then what it replaces. Present tense, active.

```
good   Claude reads the overnight inbox, sorts by what actually needs you, and
       drafts the replies you always write anyway.
weak   This powerful workflow leverages AI to help you manage your email more
       efficiently and save valuable time.
```

**Stat chip** — the honest figure. `saves 2.5 hrs`. If you cannot defend it, drop the chip.

**Verdict** — the total, and what it means in a founder's week.
`Five systems, built once. That is a working day back, every week, without hiring anyone.`

## The honesty rule

Her advantage is that her numbers are real. Protect it.

- A number on a sheet is a claim. Claims get sourced.
- Totals add up from honest parts. Never reverse-engineer the parts to hit a round total.
- If a system only saves twenty minutes, the sheet says twenty minutes. That is more
  persuasive than a rounded-up hour, because it sounds like someone who actually measured.
- Never imply a result she has not had.

## Sentence shapes that carry her voice

```
You already do this. It just does it without you now.
Built once. Runs every week.
It is not a new tool. It is the same tool, set up properly.
The part you hate is the part that automates cleanly.
Most founders try to save time by adding tools. This removes steps.
```
